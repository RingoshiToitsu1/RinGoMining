Why now: the halving cycle

Every four years or so, Bitcoin "halves," and the reward for mining new blocks gets cut in half. The last halving was in April 2024. Historically, some of Bitcoin's biggest runs came in the months and years after a halving.

We're at a point in the halving cycle where mining now means stacking Bitcoin before a potential run.

That's my view based on past cycles, not a guarantee. But here's what I like about mining in this window: you're stacking Bitcoin every single day at today's price instead of buying one lump at the top. If Bitcoin climbs, everything you mined now is worth more.

Gross vs net: the fee is real

Every GoMining miner pays a daily electricity and maintenance fee out of its rewards. On my account, on the day I checked, that fee took roughly half of the gross. Anyone telling you about GoMining profits without mentioning the fee isn't being straight with you.

The good news: you can shrink that fee.

The slow stretch, and how I stayed profitable

I'm not going to pretend it's always smooth. About six months ago, when Bitcoin's price was low, rewards were practically nothing. Plenty of people were underwater.

I stayed profitable where others weren't, for two reasons:

  1. I lock GoMining tokens for the maximum discount. Locking tokens lowers your daily maintenance fee. My discount is 28.22%, so my fees are much lower than someone who doesn't lock.
  2. Locked tokens earn their own staking rewards. Those rewards kept coming every week even when mining rewards were thin. So far they've paid me about $4,047 in total, on top of the mining.

Two income streams instead of one. When one gets thin, the other carries you.

Why not just mine at home?

I tried. I built a real facility in Rochester, Michigan. When Bitcoin fell, the power bill ate the profit. At average US electricity rates, a lot of home miners end up paying more for power than their machines earn. Then add the hardware cost, the noise, the heat, and the risk. My machines ended up in a fire in Mexico.

On GoMining, there's no hardware to buy, no power bill in your name, and nothing in your house that can burn down.
